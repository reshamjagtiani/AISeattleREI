This pulls live from Seattle & King County's public zoning, middle-housing, sewer, storm, and water GIS data, the same way I check a lot before I underwrite it. Free, no login. Built as a companion to the reel — not a substitute for a real pre-application site visit (PASV) with SDCI, SPU & SCL.

City of Seattle addresses only — this tool queries Seattle & King County systems and won't return meaningful results for other cities. Address lookup uses Seattle's official address database (with U.S. Census and OpenStreetMap as backup); parcel, zoning, transit-bonus, and utility data come directly from public government GIS services. Everything is first-pass and informational only — always verify actual field conditions and confirm with the official sources linked throughout before making decisions.

The public GIS layer includes diameter, material, and install year — but not flow rate or pressure. Those aren't published anywhere; for real numbers, request a water availability certificate from SPU (they run a hydrant flow test). Main diameter is your first clue: a 4" main feeding a 6-unit project is a very different conversation than an 8" one.

Electric service — poles, lines & capacity (250 ft)

Pole locations, heights, line routes, and structures come from Seattle City Light's public GIS. What is not public: transformer sizes, existing load on the circuit, and wire clearance heights — those only come out of City Light's engineering review. If a pole sits on your frontage, budget time for that conversation before finalizing height or placement.

Trees on & around this parcel

  • Loading…
From the city's combined tree inventory (street + city-managed + some private trees). Coverage of trees on private lots is incomplete, and the inventory doesn't flag "exceptional trees" — under Seattle's tree ordinance, trees over certain size thresholds can restrict where you build and can't just be removed. Cross-check on the city's ADUniverse property search and get an arborist survey before locking a site plan.

Side sewer sharing — not public GIS data

This is the one that actually got me: whether the side sewer (the lateral connecting this parcel's plumbing to the main) is a private line shared with the lot next door isn't published as live map data anywhere in the city's open GIS — it only exists as a scanned side sewer card on file with SPU. If you're serious about this lot, pull the card before you assume anything.
